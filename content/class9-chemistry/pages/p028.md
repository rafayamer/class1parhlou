## 2.3 Isotopes and their Masses

All the atoms of an element must necessarily have the same atomic number, but their mass number may vary depending upon the number of neutrons present in the nucleus. Atoms of the same element having different number of neutrons in their nuclei are called isotopes. For example, element carbon has three isotopes as its atoms have six, seven and eight neutrons in their nuclei. These isotopes are represented as ¹²₆C, ¹³₆C, ¹⁴₆C. Similarly, hydrogen exists as three isotopes, Hydrogen, Deuterium and Tritium represented by ¹₁H, ²₁H and ³₁H. Hydrogen (¹₁H) is the only atom which does not have a neutron. Since the chemical properties of the elements are determined by the number of electrons, all three isotopes will show almost the same chemical behaviour, although their physical properties may be different.

²₁H has twice the mass of ¹₁H while the mass of ³₁H is thrice as the mass of ¹₁H. Similarly, the masses of three different isotopes of carbon are different.
