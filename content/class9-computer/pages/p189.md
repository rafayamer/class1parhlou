• Moreover, organised data improves clarity. When data is presented clearly, it's easier to understand and interpret. For example, a chart showing monthly sales figures is much easier to understand than a long list of numbers.

To make data easy to understand, it can be organised into tables, charts, and graphs. Here's how:

**Data Tables:** Imagine you have data about students' scores in different subjects. An example of this data is shown in table 9.2.

| Student | Math | Science | English |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ali | 85 | 78 | 90 |
| Sara | 78 | 88 | 85 |
| Ahmed | 92 | 82 | 87 |
| Fatima | 90 | 80 | 89 |
| Bilal | 67 | 75 | 70 |

Table 9.2

Tables like this help present data clearly, making it easy to compare and analyse the scores of different students across various subjects.

• Bar charts

• Pie charts
