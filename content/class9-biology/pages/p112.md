## 7.3 Mechanism of Enzyme Action

An enzyme has one or more pockets or clefts on its surface. These are called active sites. The active sites are directly involved in catalysis. Two models have been proposed to explain the mechanism of enzyme action.

[FIGURE 7.2: Active site of enzyme; a blue enzyme structure with an active site labelled “Active site” and the structure labelled “Enzyme”.]

### 1- Lock and Key Model of Enzyme Action

This model was proposed by a German chemist Emil Fischer in 1894. According to it, the active site of enzyme has a fixed structure. The substrate molecule fits precisely into it to form an enzyme-substrate complex. The enzyme catalyzes the reaction and substrate is transformed into product/s. Then, the product is released from the enzyme.
