## 2.8 Gradient of a Distance-Time Graph

The gradient is the measure of slope of a line.

Consider the distance-time graph of uniform speed again. Select any two values of time t_1 and t_2. Draw two vertical dotted lines at t_1 and t_2 on x-axis. These lines meet the graph at points P and Q. From these points draw horizontal lines to meet y-axis at S_1 and S_2 respectively as shown in Fig. 2.18.

[FIGURE Fig. 2.18: Distance-time graph with a straight rising line from O through P and Q; horizontal axis labelled t (s) and x, vertical axis labelled S (m) and y; points O, P, Q and R; dashed lines marking t_1, t_2, S_1 and S_2; segments labelled (t_2 − t_1) and (S_2 − S_1); angle θ at P.]

Distance covered in this time interval is S_2 − S_1 = S  
Time taken t_2 − t_1 = t

The slope or gradient of the graph is the measure of tangent θ of the triangle RPQ:

Slope = RQ/PR

Slope = (S_2 − S_1)/(t_2 − t_1) = S/t

From Eq. (2.1), S/t = v_av, the average speed during the time interval t.

Figure 2.17 shows that S/t = tan θ = slope of graph line, therefore,

Gradient of the distance-Time graph is equal to the average speed of the body.
