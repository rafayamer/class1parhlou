## 4.1 Like and Unlike Parallel Forces

If the parallel forces are acting in the same direction, then they are called like parallel forces and if their directions are opposite to one another, they are called unlike parallel forces. Three forces F_1, F_2 and F_3 are shown in Fig. 4.1 acting on a rigid body at different points. Here, the forces F_1 and F_2 are like parallel forces but F_2 and F_3 are unlike parallel forces.

## 4.2 Addition of Forces

In chapter 2, we have learnt about vectors and their representation. Remember that the resultant is the same for any order of addition of vectors. As forces are vectors, so forces can also be added by head-to-tail rule.

To determine the resultant of two or more forces acting in a plane, the following example will explain its method.

EXAMPLE 4.1:

Let us add three force vectors F_1, F_2 and F_3, having magnitudes of 200 N, 300 N and 250 N acting at angles of 30°, 45°, 60° with x-axis.

SOLUTION:

By selecting a suitable scale 100 N = 1 cm, we can draw the force vectors as shown in Fig. 4.2(a).

To add these vectors, we apply head-to-tail rule as shown in Fig. 4.2(b).

[FIGURE Fig. 4.2 (a): Coordinate axes x and x′ horizontally, y and y′ vertically, with origin O; vectors F_1, F_2 and F_3 extend from O at angles 30°, 45° and 60° to the x-axis, respectively.]

[FIGURE Fig. 4.2 (b): Coordinate axes x and x′ horizontally, y and y′ vertically, with origin O; head-to-tail vectors F_1 (2.0 cm, 30°), F_2 (3.0 cm, 45°) and F_3 (2.5 cm, 60°); resultant F (7.1 cm) is at 43° to the x-axis.]

Measured length of resultant force is 7.1 cm. According to selected scale, magnitude of the resultant force F is 710 N and direction is at an angle 43° with x-axis.
