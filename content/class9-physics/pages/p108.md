### Units of Work

The SI unit of work is joule (J).

One joule work is done when a force of one newton acting on a body moves it through a distance of one metre in its own direction.

From Eq. (5.1)

1 J = 1 N × 1 m

or  1 J = N m

Bigger units are also used like  1 kJ = 10³ J  and  1 MJ = 10⁶ J

EXAMPLE 5.1:

A person does 200 J of work in pushing a carton through a distance of 5 metres. How much force is applied by him?

SOLUTION:

Work done  W = 200 J  
Distance  S = 5 m  
Force  F = ?  
From Eq. (5.1)  W = F × S or F = W/S  
Putting the values, we get

F = (200 J)/(5 m) = 40 N

EXAMPLE 5.2:

Find the work done by a 65 N force in pulling the suitcase (Fig. 5.8) for a distance of 20 metres.

SOLUTION:

Force applied  F = 65 N  
Distance covered  S = 20 m  
Angle from the figure  θ = 30°

[FIGURE Fig. 5.8: A person pulls a suitcase; the pulling force is shown at an angle of 30° to a horizontal dashed line.]

Work  W = ?  
Using Eq. 5.2,

W = FS cos 30°  
W = 65 N × 20 m × 0.866  
W =1125.8 Nm = 1125.8 J
