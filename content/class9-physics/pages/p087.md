EXAMPLE 4.3:

A force of 160 N is acting on a wooden box at an angle of 60° with the horizontal direction. Determine the values of its x and y components.

SOLUTION:

Magnitude of force  F = 160N  
Angle  θ = 60°  
Using calculator, sin θ = sin 60° = 0.866  
        cos θ = cos 60° = 0.5

x-component is given by Eq. (4.2)

F_x = F cos θ

Putting the values, F_x = 160 N × 0.5 = 80 N

y-component is given by Eq. (4.3)

F_y = F sin θ

Putting the values, F_y = 160 N × 0.866 = 138.6 N

## 4.6 Principle of Moments

To understand the principle of moments, let us perform an activity.

ACTIVITY 4.2

Balance a metre rule on a wedge at its centre of gravity such that the metre rule stays horizontal. Suspend two weights w_1 and w_2 on one side of the metre rule at distance l_1 and l_2 from the centre and a third weight w_3 on the other side at distance l_3 until the rule is again balanced.

[FIGURE: A horizontal metre rule supported at CG on a wedge, with weights w_1 and w_2 hanging to the left and weight w_3 hanging to the right. The distances are labelled l_1 from w_1 to CG, l_2 from w_2 to CG and l_3 from CG to w_3.]

The weights w_1 and w_2 tend to rotate the rod anticlockwise about CG and the weight w_3 tends to rotate it clockwise. The values of the moments of the weights are w_1 × l_1, w_2 × l_2 and w_3 × l_3. When the metre rule is balanced, then

Total anticlockwise moments = Total clockwise moments

w_1 × l_1 + w_2 × l_2 = w_3 × l_3 ........................(4.6)

This is known as principle of moments, which is stated as:

When a body is in balanced position, the sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about that point.
