EXAMPLE 4.4:

A metre stick is pinned at its one end O on a table so that it can rotate freely. One force of magnitude 18 N is applied perpendicular to the length of the stick at its free end. Another force of magnitude 60N is acting at an angle of 30° with the stick as shown in Fig. 4.13(a). At what distance from the end of stick that is pinned should the second force act such that the stick does not rotate?

[FIGURE Fig. 4.13(a): A horizontal metre stick is pinned at O on the left. The distance d is marked from O to the point where a 60 N force acts at 30° to the stick; a 18 N force acts downward at the free end, marked 1.0 m from O.]

SOLUTION:

Weight of the stick does not affect in the horizontal plane. Resolving force F of magnitude = 60 N into rectangular components that act at distance d from point O:

F_x = 60 N × cos 30° = 60 N × 0.866 = 51.96 N  
F_y = 60 N × sin 30° = 60 N × 0.5 = 30 N

As the component F_x passes through the axis of rotation, its torque is zero. Torque τ₁ of 30 N is positive and τ₂ of 18 N force is negative. The stick will not rotate when these two torques balance each other, i.e τ₁ = τ₂ or F_y × d = F_y′ × d′

30 N × d = 18 N × 1 m

d = (18 N × 1 m)/30 N = 0.6 m

## 4.7 Centre of Gravity and Centre of Mass

An object is composed of a large number of small particles. Each particle is acted upon by the gravitational force directed towards the centre of the Earth (Fig. 4.14-a). As the object is small as compared to the Earth, the value of g can be taken as uniform over all particles. Therefore, each particle experiences the same force mg. Since all these forces are parallel and act in the same direction, so their resultant as shown in Fig. 4.14(b) will be equal to the sum of all these forces i.e.

Resultant force = Σ mg where Σ means the “sum of”.
